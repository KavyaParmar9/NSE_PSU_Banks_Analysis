    Chapter §3 · Tail risk

    Value at Risk and Expected Shortfall

    One-day losses for our two risk stocks: SBIN, the sector's large anchor, and UNIONBANK. VaR is the loss not exceeded on 95 or 99 days in 100; ES is the average loss on the days that do exceed it.

    Loss thresholds by measure

    SBINUNIONBANK
    Show every period and method as a table

    Where the tail starts

    Daily returns, 2020–2025, with fitted Student's t
    • UNIONBANK is riskier on every measure and in every period. Full-sample historical 99% VaR is 7.17% against SBIN's 5.41%.
    • 99% VaR is roughly double 95% VaR for both stocks, a direct sign of fat tails. The fitted t has only about 3 degrees of freedom.
    • Risk fell sharply after 2022. SBIN's 95% VaR dropped from 3.65% (2020–22, which holds the COVID crash) to 2.00% (2023–25).
    • An assumed-df=5 parametric t is the most conservative (SBIN 99% VaR 6.49%), because it uses the sample standard deviation as its scale. We rely on the historical and fitted-t estimates.

    Chapter §4 · Stress test

    March 2020 stress test

    We added the 21 trading days of March 2020 to the calm 2023–2025 sample, about 3% more observations, and re-ran every measure.

    Change from adding one crisis month

    2023–25 → 2023–25 + Mar 2020, historical method
    SBINUNIONBANK
    • The 95% level barely moves. SBIN 95% VaR rises 9%, from 2.00% to 2.18%. Ordinary bad days are unchanged.
    • The 99% tail reacts strongly. SBIN 99% VaR rises 44% (3.54% → 5.09%) and 99% ES rises 62% (5.56% → 8.99%).
    • UNIONBANK moves less in the historical method (+22% on 99% ES) because its tail was already fat, but its fitted-t 99% ES jumps 66%, from 7.60% to 12.64%.
    • The lesson: three calm years can hide how bad a single month gets. Tail measures need crisis data in the sample, or a stress overlay.

    Chapter §5 · Volatility

    Volatility that remembers

    EWMA variance, σ²t = λσ²t−1 + (1−λ)r²t−1, with λ estimated by maximum likelihood under Student's t errors, compared with an equal-weight rolling window sized to cover 90% of EWMA's weight.

    Annualised conditional volatility

    Does EWMA forecast tomorrow?

    OLS of r²t+1 on σ̂²t. A perfect forecast has α = 0 and η = 1.
    • SBIN's volatility is persistent (λ = 0.978, half-life ≈ 31 trading days). UNIONBANK's reacts to shocks within days (λ = 0.928, half-life ≈ 9).
    • Neither matches the RiskMetrics default of 0.94, so one fixed λ would not suit both banks.
    • η is significant for both, so EWMA carries real information. SBIN is close to unbiased; UNIONBANK's η of 0.44 shows its forecasts overshoot.
    • EWMA edges the rolling window on RMSE for both stocks, by a margin too small to call it clearly better.

    Chapter §6 · Portfolio optimisation

    Building the quietest portfolio

    Mean-variance optimisation across all 12 banks, using annualised historical means and four covariance estimators, with and without short selling.

    Efficient frontier, long only

    Sample covariance. Hover the curve to see the weights.
    Efficient frontierSingle bankMinimum variance

    Minimum-variance weights

    • SBIN anchors every solution, from 66% (Ledoit-Wolf, long only) to 99% (EWMA, shorts allowed), because it has the lowest standalone volatility at 31%.
    • Long-only gives corner solutions. Five banks get zero weight under sample covariance: BANKBARODA, CANBK, CENTRALBK, IOB and PSB.
    • Allowing shorts trims volatility only slightly, from 29.26% to 28.95% under sample covariance, by shorting CANBK (−14.9%) and BANKBARODA (−6.8%). Borrow costs on PSU names would likely erase that gain.
    • EWMA covariance reports far lower risk (16.2%) because it weights the calmer recent years. Shrinkage and OAS barely differ from the sample estimate.

    Chapter §7 · Factor exposure

    What drives the returns

    Carhart four-factor regressions on excess returns, using the IIM Ahmedabad Indian factor library (market, size SMB, value HML, momentum WML), and CAPM betas estimated separately for each half of the sample.

    Four-factor betas, 2020–2025

    Green is positive exposure, terracotta is negative.

    CAPM beta, then and now

    2020–22 → 2023–25, sorted by change
    2020–20222023–2025
    • Every bank loads heavily on value (HML 0.50–0.98), and smaller banks such as IOB, CENTRALBK and MAHABANK also carry strong size exposure (SMB ≈ 0.9).
    • SBIN behaves like a large cap: its size beta is the only negative one (−0.08) and its value beta is the lowest (0.50).
    • Momentum exposure is mostly negative, so these stocks tended to lag when recent winners kept winning.
    • Market sensitivity rose for most banks after 2022. PSB, CENTRALBK and UCOBANK added almost a full point of beta. SBIN, INDIANB and CANBK became slightly less sensitive.

    Chapter §8 · Summary

    Key findings

    What we would tell a risk manager or an allocator looking at this sector.

    31.1%

    SBIN is the sector's stabiliser

    Lowest annualised volatility, lowest VaR and ES, and the largest weight in every minimum-variance portfolio.

    +62%

    Tail risk hides in calm samples

    Adding March 2020 to 2023–25 raised SBIN's 99% ES by 62% while its 95% VaR moved only 9%.

    31 vs 9

    Volatility memory differs by bank

    Half-life in trading days: SBIN's λ of 0.978 against UNIONBANK's 0.928. A single RiskMetrics λ fits neither.

    29.3%

    Diversification inside the sector is thin

    Pairwise correlations run from about 0.4 to 0.8, so even the long-only minimum-variance portfolio keeps 29.3% volatility.

    −0.3 pts

    Short selling buys very little

    Shorting cuts minimum-variance volatility from 29.26% to 28.95%, small enough that borrow costs and squeeze risk outweigh it.

    9 of 12

    Value stocks with rising beta

    Strong HML loadings throughout, and market betas that rose for nine of the twelve banks between the two sub-periods.
